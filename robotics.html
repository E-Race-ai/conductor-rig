PRJ-04 · Robotics · RB-SYS-001 · Rev B · 2026-11-19

RoboticsThe robot fleet for staff augmentation: hardware, vendors, payload modules, maintenance and fleet operations

Robotics is its own project: it selects, buys, deploys and maintains the robot fleet that augments every department. Robots take the heavy, repetitive and long-distance work (linen, luggage, supplies, waste, small-item delivery, overnight floor care) so staff spend their time with guests. Each robot navigates on its own onboard LiDAR SLAM; Hotel.OS assigns its tasks; the digital twin is the map both share.

Vendors under evaluation: Pudu Robotics · Keenon Robotics

RB-SYS-001 · 1.0

Concept: augment the team, do not replace it

In a 1,000,000 sq ft building most staff hours are spent walking and pushing carts between back of house and the floors. The augmentation layer moves that load onto robots and leaves people where they matter: with guests, and on tasks that need hands, judgement or a conversation.

RB-SYS-001 · 2.0

Floor care: the largest share of robot hours

In a building this size most robot hours go to floors, not deliveries: lobby and retail stone, carpeted corridors and ballrooms, back-of-house vinyl, restrooms and the garage, cleaned every night in the hours guests are asleep. Nine of the planned fleet are floor-care robots. Each runs its own zones on a schedule, docks itself, and reports coverage back to Hotel.OS, so the twin shows what has been cleaned and what has not.

RB-SYS-001 · 3.0

Logistics: wheel base plus payload modules

Heavy-load robots are treated as a common wheel platform. What changes from task to task is the module on top: a bin, a shelf, a cart or a locked cabinet. Lifting bases drive under a module and raise it (60 mm on the Pudu T-series, rack-lift on the Keenon S300); fixed-deck bases carry a mounted module (Keenon S100). Hotel.OS always knows which module is on which robot.

3.2Small-item delivery and brand wraps: Keenon W3

The program team has selected the Keenon W3 for small-item delivery to guest doors. Its 2–4 locked compartments (about 390 × 385 × 300 mm each, 10 kg per compartment, 20 kg total) suit the requests that make up most of a front desk's runs. Each W3 body is also visible, moving brand space in corridors, lobbies and elevators: it can carry the property's identity or a corporate sponsor's wrap.

3.3Lobby smart market + W3: BistroBox

Purchases are charged to a card or, with authorisation, to the guest's room.

One robot then serves a pool of 20–30 racks.

RB-SYS-001 · 5.0

Dispatch and control through Hotel.OS

Staff never run a vendor app. A task created in Hotel.OS (by a person, a guest request or a schedule) names a destination on the twin, a module and a priority; Hotel.OS picks the robot and dispatches it through a fleet adapter. Status, completion and exceptions come back to the same map and task.
