What the extra $13,503 would have bought

Why A

Most of the power for 40% of the price

  • Our agents mostly call cloud models, so we don't need a giant local GPU on day one
  • Same server-class platform: 7 full-size card slots, 128 PCIe lanes, 10 GbE and remote management
  • Same Kioxia drives as B, so storage is identical
  • Saves $13,503 now, to spend later only when we need it
What we give up, for now

Half the cores, memory and VRAM

  • 16 cores instead of 32
  • 128 GB instead of 256 GB: about 14 agents at once instead of 33
  • 16 GB of GPU memory instead of 48 GB, so 30B–70B models run slower, partly from system memory
How we close the gap

Upgrade in this order

  • 1. Memory: fill the 4 empty slots for 256 GB and twice the bandwidth
  • 2. GPU: add or swap in a 48 GB card; the board has 4 full x16 slots
  • 3. Power: a bigger PSU only when a second big GPU goes in

The silicon

CPU: EPYC 9175F topology

AMD builds the EPYC 9175F an unusual way: sixteen Zen 5 compute dies with just one core switched on in each, around a single I/O die. Every core gets its own 32 MB of L3 cache and its own link to memory. It is AMD's high-frequency part for databases and GPU host machines.

  • 5.0 GHzBoost clock on a 4.2 GHz base, at 320 W.
  • 512 MBL3 cache: 32 MB for every single core.
  • 16Memory links, one per compute die, so the CPU can saturate whatever memory we install.
  • 512-bitFull-width AVX-512. Vector math runs in one pass, not two halves.
  • 12 → 4Memory channels: the CPU is rated for 12 (614 GB/s). This board wires 8, and we fill 4 today.

Sources: AMD, Level1Techs, PassMark, ASRock Rack spec sheet (via Central Computer).

The orchestra

Agent concurrency model

~14

coding agents running at once, our estimate. Six agents measured ~43.5 GB of RAM, about 7 GB each. 128 GB leaves room for ~14 after the OS and services, and ~33 once we add four more memory sticks.

Memory runs out first in agent work. Every session starts its own tool servers, browsers and containers. That is why memory is the first upgrade on our list: four empty slots are waiting.

Local models take the routine calls. A fast 20B-class model handles routing, tool calls and fallback on the 16 GB GPU, or an embedding and reranking pair for search. Frontier work goes to cloud models like Claude.

Power & air

Thermal and power budget

Cool air enters through three 140 mm fans in front. It crosses the board and the GPU, then leaves through the rear fan and the 360 mm radiator on top, which cools the CPU. Two reverse-flow fans in the shroud push cool air up into the GPU. All six case fans come with the Antec 900.

~850 Wfull-load estimate at the wall, of a 1,200 W PSU
~7.1 Aof a standard 15 A, 120 V circuit
~350 Wspare PSU capacity for upgrades (80 Plus Platinum)

Wall power is an estimate from component ratings: 320 W CPU + 300 W GPU + ~150 W for memory, drives, fans and board ≈ 770 W, plus PSU losses. A UPS of 1,500 VA is tight; 2,200 VA is recommended. Sources: AMD, NVIDIA, Super Flower, Antec, Gamers Nexus.
