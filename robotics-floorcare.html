Floor careRobot profiles for overnight floor care at The Grand: Pudu CC1 Pro, Pudu MT1 family, Keenon C40 and C30

Floor care takes the largest share of robot hours at The Grand, Miami (≈ 1,000,000 sq ft of condominium, two hotel operations and lobby retail). The planned set is nine robots: four Pudu CC1 Pro scrubbers for lobby stone, restrooms and back of house, four Pudu MT1 Vac units for carpeted corridors and ballrooms, and one Pudu MT1 sweeper for the garage and dock. The Keenon C40 is the main alternative scrubber; the Keenon C30 is the smaller dry-only option. All vendors are candidates under evaluation. Almost every figure on this page is a vendor or reseller claim; no independent hands-on test of any of these robots was found.

RB-FC-001 · 2.0

Pudu CC1 Pro

A compact 4-in-1 commercial cleaning robot (model CCBC02) announced on 27 May 2025. It keeps the chassis, battery, tanks and runtimes of the original CC1 (May 2023) and adds an AI layer: a rear verification camera, AI spot scrubbing, liquid-stain and floor-type detection, brush and squeegee self-monitoring and expanded VSLAM+ mapping. The Pro premium pays for perception and reporting, not for more capacity or speed. Pudu states over 20,000 CC1-platform units sold.

Strengths

  • Only planned robot that wet-scrubs and dries hard floors; recovery tank equals clean tank (15 / 15 L).
  • Rear camera, stain map and heat map give cleaning evidence per run, useful for public lobby and retail frontage.
  • Documented compliance trail is the strongest of the set: IEC 63327 claim, FCC and ISED statements, IPX4.
  • PUDU Open Platform gives Hotel.OS a task-level dispatch path.
  • Mature chassis: CC1 platform shipping since 2023 at scale (vendor claim).

Limitations and known issues

  • No independent test or hands-on review found; all performance figures are vendor claims.
  • Small tanks: a large lobby needs several dock cycles a night, so the workstation is effectively required for unattended runs.
  • 8 mm threshold while cleaning, 3° cleaning slope, 75 cm preferred passage: elevator sills and ramps need floor-by-floor checks.
  • Short-pile carpet only; thick corridor carpet is outside its envelope. A reseller page reports the CC1 struggles with thick carpet and high thresholds.
  • Pudu's cloud robot-management API had a cross-tenant authorisation flaw (disclosed Aug 2025, fixed within days, no CVE); the CC1 was among the named models.
  • Conflicting runtime: RobotLAB advertises "up to 8 hours" against the manual's 5 h scrub.

Fit for The Grand

  • Planned × 4: lobby and retail stone, restrooms, back-of-house vinyl and epoxy.
  • At vendor maximum, one robot covers 3,500–5,000 m² per 5 h scrub charge; four cover about 14,000–20,000 m² per charge cycle. Real rates in furnished space are lower and are measured in the pilot.
  • Glass storefronts on the retail frontage: test sensors before relying on them.
  • Florida reseller channel exists (Tovrana/Sparkobot); no Pudu-authorised South Florida technician confirmed.

Open questions for the vendor

  1. FCC ID and grant date for CCBC02; confirm authorisation before 28 Jul 2026 (FCC DA 26-786).
  2. Workstation water supply, drain, power and footprint; self-cleaning vs standard dock.
  3. Squeegee width, turning width and passing height for the Pro (CC1 values: 63 cm, 88 cm, 76 cm).
  4. Elevator control kit: SKU, price, supported OEMs at The Grand; maps and floors per robot.
  5. Open Platform scope for the CC1 Pro: task start and stop, schedules, map areas, elevator events, webhooks, rate limits, private-cloud option.
  6. TÜV SÜD certificate number and standard; IEC 63327 test report; camera-image retention and data residency.
  7. US hotel references running the CC1 Pro (not the CC1); warranty term and service SLA.

Pudu MT1 family: MT1, MT1 Vac, MT1 Max

Three dry-only machines on a shared 840 × 600 mm footprint: the MT1 sweeper (10 Sep 2024), the MT1 Vac sweeper-vacuum (July 2025, model MTBC02) and the MT1 Max 3D-LiDAR sweeper for semi-outdoor use (20 Aug 2025, model MTBC03). None of them scrubs. Pudu positions the MT1 Vac between the wet CC1 series and the dry MT1. The MT1 Vac is the only variant built for carpet; the MT1 and MT1 Max are for large hard floors, garages and covered drives.

Strengths

  • MT1 Vac is the only planned robot built for carpet; floor-type recognition adjusts suction and brush speed.
  • H11 filtration with an H13 option for guest corridors and ballrooms.
  • Vendor claim: up to 10 days in a 1,000 m² carpeted area before emptying.
  • MT1 has the highest rate of the planned set (1,800 m²/h) and a 35 L bin; auto-empty available.
  • AI litter detection and spot mode suit patrols of busy areas between full sweeps.
  • Same Pudu cloud and API as the CC1 Pro: one vendor integration for the planned fleet.

Limitations and known issues

  • Dry-only. No variant can lift wet spills or scrub.
  • Perception can struggle with black, mirrored or transparent surfaces (reported in a desk review of the Max guide); expect mapping work around storefront glass.
  • Large footprint: 75 cm minimum passage, 80–85 cm recommended; not for guest rooms or residential corridors with doors.
  • MT1 Vac: no auto-empty; dust bag and air filter are weekly changes, about 52 of each per robot per year.
  • MT1 Vac carpet pile limit is "short-pile and industrial" only, with no height in mm.
  • Base MT1 noise, slope, threshold and IP rating not published (operation guide not retrieved).
  • No independent tests and no named US deployment with measured results.

Fit for The Grand

  • MT1 Vac × 4: carpeted guest corridors, ballrooms and meeting levels, overnight and after event strike. At vendor maximum one robot covers 4,200–9,100 m² per charge.
  • MT1 × 1: garage and loading dock. Ramps above 3° are excluded from cleaning; at vendor maximum 7,200–14,400 m² per charge.
  • MT1 Max (alternative): covered drives and porte-cochère, where IP54 and the warning beacon matter.
  • Check elevator sills, transition strips and airwall tracks against the 20 mm limit.

Open questions for the vendor

  1. FCC IDs and grant dates for MT1, MTBC02 and MTBC03 (pre-28 Jul 2026 authorisation required).
  2. MT1 Vac maximum carpet pile height and performance on hotel cut and loop pile.
  3. Base MT1 threshold, slope, noise and IP rating; whether it may run in an open-air garage level.
  4. Elevator-control kit for MT1 / MT1 Vac: price, supported OEMs, per-floor map switching.
  5. Keep-out zones and virtual walls in the MT1 mapping tools.
  6. Auto-Empty Station compatibility with the MT1 Vac (not listed); charging-station price.
  7. Consumable unit prices (bags, filters, brushes) and warranty term.

Keenon KLEENBOT C40

A 4-in-1 commercial cleaner (sweep, vacuum, scrub, dust push) with a triple-brush system, announced on 31 Mar 2025 with the C55 and C20. Keenon positions it for mid-size supermarkets, hotels, offices and hospitals and highlights a dry-wet separation design to limit odour from mixed waste. A technology upgrade was announced in March 2026 with no public changelog. It is the most direct like-for-like alternative to the CC1 Pro. No independent hands-on or lab test was found.

Strengths

  • Narrowest passage of the scrubbers (65 cm vs 70 cm for the CC1 Pro).
  • Larger clean tank (16 L) and dust bag (8 L); 5-second battery swap allows a second shift without waiting on charge.
  • Shorter charge (≈ 2 h) and higher stated suction (23,500 Pa).
  • Lower street price, about $4–5k less per unit than the CC1 Pro.
  • One vendor cloud if the property standardises on Keenon delivery robots.

Limitations and known issues

  • No public API or SDK; Hotel.OS dispatch would need a negotiated Keenon or integrator agreement.
  • No US certification documentation found (FCC, UL, CE); FCC authorisation status unverified.
  • Sensors may misbehave where walls are largely glass or transparent (user manual).
  • 15 mm obstacle limit; carpet under 10 mm pile only; indoor only, no IP rating.
  • Inconsistent documentation: waste tank 11 L vs 14 L, a "60 L / 47 L" line, runtime figures that differ between page and manual.
  • 1,100 m²/h is a geometric ceiling (400 mm × 0.8 m/s ≈ 1,150 m²/h); real scrub throughput is far lower.
  • No US hotel deployment found; the named hotel site (Shangri-La Traders, Shanghai Hongqiao) has no published KPIs.

Fit for The Grand

  • Alternative to the CC1 Pro for lobby, restroom and back-of-house scrubbing; not in the current plan.
  • Best case is corridors, residential lobbies and retail frontage; Keenon's C55 is the larger model for big open floor plates.
  • Glass-heavy lobby frontage and elevator-sill gaps need a site acceptance test.
  • Selection would require written API access and FCC authorisation evidence first.

Open questions for the vendor

  1. FCC ID and grant date; UL 3300 or UL 1017 / 60335-2-72 status; declaration of conformity.
  2. Cloud API access for task dispatch, status webhooks, map and zone IDs; data residency and camera-image handling; offline option.
  3. Confirmed waste-tank volume and runtime for the current (2026 upgrade) hardware; contents of the upgrade.
  4. Workstation plumbing, drain and footprint; price with and without workstation.
  5. Cleaning slope limit; per-floor maps, keep-out zones, scheduling.
  6. US elevator integration reference; US hotel, airport or mall reference sites; Florida service coverage.
  7. Warranty term from Keenon, consumable prices.

The C40 is cheaper by roughly $4–5k per unit, fits a 65 cm passage, carries more clean water and swaps batteries in seconds; it becomes a valid choice only if Keenon provides written API access and FCC and safety documentation, or if the property standardises on Keenon delivery robots. Both need a site trial on the lobby stone, glass frontage and elevator sills before purchase.

8.4What staff still do

Every night

  • Clear cables, signage and loose items from robot zones before runs.
  • Empty dust bins and bags; detergent top-up where no workstation dosing.
  • Respond to stuck or error alerts; a robot waits at its maintenance point.
  • Spills during service, spot treatment, edges and corners, stairs and escalators, glass, guest rooms.
  • Move furniture for ballroom runs after event strike.

Weekly and by exception

  • Sensor and LiDAR cleaning, wheel wipe-down, tank flushes.
  • Consumable swaps on schedule; track stock by robot.
  • Review heat maps and missed-area reports; adjust zones in Hotel.OS.
  • During fire recall, elevators return to the recall floor; staff clear any robot from lobby egress paths Inference.
Regulatory gateFCC Public Notice DA 26-786 (28 Jul 2026) added foreign-produced advanced robotic devices to the Covered List. Models authorised before that date may still be imported, sold and used, and a waiver (DA 26-789) allows security and compatibility software updates until at least 1 Jan 2029. Before purchase, verify the FCC ID and grant date for each model, and contract for spare parts, batteries and firmware support through 2029 and beyond.
