PRJ-03 · Hotel.OS · HO-SYS-001 · Rev B · 2026-11-19

Hotel.OSHotel operating system: one digital twin, one task model, every department

Hotel.OS is the orchestration layer for everything that happens in a building, by people and by robots. Each robot navigates locally on its own LiDAR SLAM; the digital twin gives robots and people one shared model of the building, mapped down to every switch and outlet; Hotel.OS assigns and tracks every task on it. Everyone sees the whole building, whatever their department and wherever they are, while their own role stays in focus.

    HO-SYS-001 · 1.0

    Purpose and scope

    A full-service hotel runs on a dozen disconnected systems — PMS, POS, work orders, messaging, radios, access control — plus, increasingly, robots from several vendors. Requests cross departments by phone and radio; robots run their own apps; nobody has one view of the work. Hotel.OS is the operating layer above those systems: it does not replace the PMS or POS, it connects them, turns every request into one kind of task, and dispatches that task to the right person, device or robot.

    In scopeOut of scope
    Cross-department task and workflow engine with SLAsReplacing the PMS, POS, payment processor or accounting system
    Staff communication: messaging, push-to-talk, escalationGuest-facing booking engine
    Multi-vendor robot dispatch, including doors and elevatorsRobot navigation stacks (owned by each robot vendor)
    Integration bus to existing hotel systemsStoring card data (payments stay tokenised at the processor)
    AI engine for intake, dispatch, forecasting and staff assistanceAutonomous AI actions that charge or move guests without approval
    Operational analytics and KPI measurementBuilding management (HVAC/BMS) control; read-only telemetry later
    HO-SYS-001 · 2.0

    System context

    Hotel.OS sits between people, devices and robots on one side and the property's existing systems on the other. The spatial model (spaces, routes, doors, elevators) is imported from Site Capture, so tasks and robots are located on surveyed geometry rather than hand-drawn maps.

    2.1Orchestration architecture: three layers

    Every robot already navigates on its own: onboard LiDAR SLAM handles localisation, path planning and obstacle avoidance in the corridor it is in. What a building with several robot fleets and seven departments lacks is a shared picture and one place that decides the work. Hotel.OS separates those concerns into three layers.

    LayerOwnsDoes not ownInterface
    L3 · Orchestration (Hotel.OS)All tasks, human and robotic; assignment, scheduling, SLAs; elevator and door arbitration across vendors; exceptions; auditMotion control, local obstacle avoidanceFleet adapters (Open-RMF), mobile devices, integrations
    L2 · Shared world model (twin)One survey frame: geometry, room numbering, objects and assets, route graph, keep-outs, doors, lifts, docks; live state of every task, robot and personDecisions; per-vendor navigation mapsMap alignment transforms; twin APIs
    L1 · Local autonomyEach robot: SLAM localisation, path planning, obstacle avoidance, safety stops. Each person: judgement, hands, guest interactionTask priority, cross-fleet coordinationVendor cloud / private APIs; devices
    Map alignmentEvery vendor keeps its own SLAM map; Hotel.OS registers each one to the twin frame with a per-map transform fitted on surveyed anchor points (doors, columns, lift sills), and re-fits it whenever the vendor re-maps. Destinations are sent in the robot's own frame; poses come back and are shown in the twin frame. Target alignment residual ≤ 10 cm (est.).

    2.2Shared view, role focus

    Everyone sees the same building, wherever they are and whichever department they work for: every open task, robot, alarm and person on shift, on one map. Each role opens it through its own lens: its own work in focus, everything else visible as context. Try the lens in the live model above.

    RoleIn focusVisible as context
    General managerEverything: whole-building load, SLAs, incidents, staffing—
    HousekeepingRoom status, turnovers, linen and amenity runs, linen robotsMaintenance in the same room, security holds, VIP arrivals
    FacilitiesWork orders pinned to objects, asset history, technician routesOccupancy and housekeeping in the room before entering
    SecurityDoors, alarms, incidents, patrolsWho and which robot is on the floor right now
    Robotics operationsFleet health, exceptions, charging, elevator queuesHuman tasks the robots are supporting
    Front deskGuest requests, delivery ETAs, room readinessEverything blocking a room from being ready
    HO-SYS-001 · 3.0

    Building digital twin and spatial operations

    Hotel.OS turns the Site Capture survey into an object-level digital twin of the whole building, down to every light switch, outlet and fixture, and then runs operations on it. Facilities, housekeeping, security and robotics work from one map of the same building: every task, robot and team member is located on surveyed geometry, so in a 1,000,000 sq ft property nobody has to describe where something is.

    3.6Walk the property: imagery, LiDAR and facilities plans in one frame

    Every square metre of in-scope space can be walked digitally. The walkthrough is not a separate photo tour: each 360° and 4K frame carries its capture pose in the survey frame, so any pixel the user selects resolves through the LiDAR depth to a 3D coordinate, a floor, a room and the nearest asset. Facilities floor plans supply the names and numbering the building already uses.

    LayerSource and methodFidelity / accuracyStatus

    3.8General manager’s view

    The GM sees the whole building at once: every floor, every open task, every robot, every alarm and staff coverage, with department KPIs alongside. Any item drills down from building to floor to room, and opens the walkthrough at the exact place the task concerns. Department leads see the same model filtered to their work; nobody runs a separate map.

    HO-SYS-001 · 7.0

    Robot fleet orchestration

    Properties will run robots from more than one vendor. Hotel.OS treats every robot as an assignee for tasks and delegates navigation to the vendor stack, coordinating them through Open-RMF (the open-source ROS 2 framework for multi-fleet interoperability, with fleet, door and lift adapters and traffic management).

The door and elevator inventory from Site Capture (FU-SYS-001 S4) is the input to this per-site integration plan. Manual fallback (staff escort) is always defined.
    Robots: PRJ-04 RoboticsThe robots this section dispatches belong to a separate project, Robotics, which owns the fleet: heavy-load wheel platforms with swappable payload modules, the Keenon W3 for small-item delivery and brand wraps, and floor-care robots, with vendor API, FCC and elevator requirements.
